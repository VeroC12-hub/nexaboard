# Where the teaching decisions in this folder come from

Outside sources that a brief in this folder cites. Kept separately so a brief
can say "see sources" rather than carry a reading list, and so a later session
can check whether a claim was actually read or merely assumed.

Nothing here is quoted at length. These are structural findings: how a
published textbook sequences a topic, and what the research says about
sequencing it the other way round.

## The textbook sequence

- OpenStax, *Algebra and Trigonometry 2e*, section 6.3, Logarithmic
  Functions.
  https://openstax.org/books/algebra-and-trigonometry-2e/pages/6-3-logarithmic-functions

  Read 2026-11-03 for `teaching-from-zero.md`. The section's skeleton, in
  order: objectives, a real situation (two earthquakes on the Richter scale),
  a problem with no method available yet, a graph, a bridge from the inverse
  functions the reader already knows, notation as its own stage, then the
  definition in a box, a misconception note, a two-step how-to, and from
  there a repeating cycle of concept, rule, worked example, matching practice
  item, closing by returning to the earthquake. The definition is the seventh
  thing on the page. Openly licensed, which is why this one and not a
  commercial series: it can be read and re-read by any later session.

## What happens when the definition comes first

- *The history of logarithms and its didactical implications*, Educational
  Sciences journal (uoc.gr).
  https://ejournals.lib.uoc.gr/edusci/article/view/1753

  Reports that textbooks long opened with Euler's definition, and describes
  the usual introduction as arriving out of the blue with little connection
  to what students have already learned.

- Brazilian textbook studies on logarithm teaching, via
  https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/45/45135/tde-26102017-173929/en.php
  and https://bdtd.ibict.br/vufind/Record/UFSM_4b08336ae0df009c23fad7c9ba991e32

  Report that most textbooks in that tradition present the logarithm as the
  solution of an exponential equation, that this definition is abstract
  enough to obstruct learning, and that the properties arriving after it are
  read by students as rules imposed on them and often unproven.

- A comparison of exponent and logarithm chapters in Taiwanese and Indonesian
  textbooks. https://papers.iafor.org/submission10813

  Listed as a further source on how different traditions sequence the same
  material. Not yet read.

## A caution on all of the above

These are studies of mathematics textbooks, mostly about one topic. They are
good evidence that opening with a formal definition is a known failure and
that textbooks put a reason before it. They are not evidence about Ghanaian
classrooms, about this app's learners, or about subjects outside mathematics.
Where a brief in this folder generalises from them, it says so.
