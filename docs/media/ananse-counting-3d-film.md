# Ananse Counts the Mangoes

**A 3D animated counting film, production pack**
NexaCore Innovations · NEXA-EDU · prepared 11 October 2026

Source storyboard: `video/ananse/lessons/count-1-to-10/lesson.ts` (the five scene
Remotion lesson). This pack turns that 2D lesson into a 3D film of about 2 minutes
40 seconds, keeps every teaching line word for word, and adds a story around it.

---

## 1. At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Title | Ananse Counts the Mangoes |
| Audience | Creche, nursery and KG (ages 3 to 6) |
| Learning goal | Say the numbers 1 to 10 in order, match each spoken number to one object, and recognise 3, 5 and 10 as groups |
| Second goal | Sharing (Ananse is caught being greedy, as always) |
| Runtime | 2:40 film plus a 6 second end card |
| Format | 16:9, 1920×1080, 25 fps master; a 9:16 cut for Shorts and WhatsApp Status |
| Style | Soft stylised 3D, warm golden hour, plush toy materials |
| Voice | English, Ghanaian accent: Ananse (lead) and Mama Yaa (two off screen lines) |
| Setting | A Ghanaian family compound with a mango tree |

### What changed from the storyboard, and why

- **Kept word for word:** every line in `lesson.ts` (hello, here come the
  mangoes, the three groups, count again, ten mangoes). Those lines are already
  timed and tested with children; the film should not teach differently from the game.
- **Added a story wrapper:** Mama Yaa's promise at the start, and Ananse trying
  to keep all ten at the end. Ananse's whole character is "greedy, always
  caught", and a 2:40 film needs a reason to watch to the end.
- **Changed the handover:** the game line "Count the mangoes and feed Ananse!"
  becomes a finger counting call to action, because a video has no game after it.
- **Added Ghanaian greetings:** *Akwaaba* (welcome) at the start, *Ayekoo*
  (well done) at the end. Both are understood by children across Ghana.
- **No people on screen.** Mama Yaa is only a voice, matching the rule in
  `public/ananse/art/PROMPTS.md`: no painted person decides what a child "should" look like.

### The one rule that shapes the whole workflow

**Numbers, numerals and mango counts are never generated by an AI video model.**
As `docs/video.md` records, Kling, Veo, Runway and the rest cannot reliably
write a numeral or keep exactly seven mangoes on screen. A counting film that
shows 6 mangoes while saying "seven" is worse than no film. So:

- AI generates the **world and the acting**: the compound, the tree, Ananse moving and talking.
- Every **counted mango and every numeral** is composited on top in Remotion
  (already in this repo) or DaVinci Resolve, from one approved mango asset.

## 2. Style bible

### Look

Soft, rounded, stylised 3D like a modern family animated feature. Nothing
sharp, nothing realistic. Materials feel like felt, clay and plush. Warm late
afternoon sun from the **upper left** in every shot (same as the game art, so the
film and the game feel like one world). Gentle depth of field, soft ambient occlusion.

### Palette (taken from `public/ananse/rig.js`)

|---|---|---|
| Ananse body | warm gold | `#F2A83C` (highlight `#FFD07A`, shadow `#C2701A`) |
| Ananse legs | soft brown | `#7A4420` (tips `#A9653A`) |
| Outline and ink | dark brown | `#32200F` |
| Kente sash bands | black, red, yellow, green, black | `#1D1A17` `#D6402E` `#F2D024` `#0F8A4D` `#1D1A17` |

| Mango | orange red blush, yellow green shoulder | `#F36A2B` to `#B9C33A` |

### Ananse, the character

- One big round golden body, a smaller round head, **eight** short, soft,
  rounded legs. Plush toy proportions, about two heads tall.
- Huge eyes with large dark pupils and white highlights, expressive brows, small mouth.
- A **diagonal** kente sash across the body (diagonal on purpose, see the
  comment in `rig.js`: a horizontal band turns him into a ball).
- A small red cap with a gold stripe and gold bobble that swings a little late when he moves.
- Never: fangs, hair, more than eight legs, real spider textures, dark lighting.
- He is never completely still. Breathing, a small bob, blinking.

### His seven faces (same names as `rig.js` MOODS)

| Mood | Used in | Look |
|---|---|---|
| `hello` | Scene 2 | open friendly smile, relaxed |
| `hungry` | Scene 3 | wide eyes, mouth open, leaning towards the tree |
| `watching` | Scenes 4, 5 | focused, slightly lifted brows, following each mango |
| `happy` | Scenes 5, 6, 8 | big bounce, eyes crinkled, mouth wide |
| `scheming` | Scene 7 | narrowed eyes, low brows, sly half smile |
| `caught` | Scene 7 | eyes huge, brows up, body squashed down, mouth a wobbly "o" |
| `sleepy` | not used | |

### The hero mango

One mango design for the whole film: plump, kidney shaped, orange red blush
fading to yellow green at the stem, one small leaf on a short stem, soft
satin sheen. It is rendered **once** as a transparent PNG (and a short
"squash on landing" sequence) and reused for every counted mango, so all ten
are identical and countable.

### Scene 4 · Here come the mangoes · 0:42 to 1:12

The core of the film. **The mangoes in this scene are composited, not generated.**

**Shots**
1. 0:42 to 0:47 · Ananse runs to the tree and gives the trunk a big hug and a
   shake. The canopy shakes. Mood `happy`.
2. 0:47 to 1:12 · One locked off wide shot of the open ground. A clean, empty
   background plate. Mangoes drop in one at a time from the top of frame, land
   with a small squash and settle in a **ten frame**: a top row of five, then a
   bottom row of five. Ananse sits on the left edge, head turning to follow
   each one (mood `watching`).

**Narration (Ananse)**
> Here come the mangoes. Count them with me.
> One. *(beat)* Two. *(beat)* Three. *(beat)* Four. *(beat)* Five.
> Six. *(beat)* Seven. *(beat)* Eight. *(beat)* Nine. *(beat)* Ten.

*Delivery:* one number per mango, about **2.2 seconds apart**. Each number is
said **as the mango lands**, never before. Leave the gap: the child should have
time to say it too. A small lift in pitch on each number, warmest on "Ten".

**On screen (composited)**
- Each mango lands, and its numeral pops up above it (1, 2, 3...) in the app font,
  white with a dark brown outline, then shrinks to a small label that stays.
- A large running numeral in the top right shows the current count.
- Layout: two rows of five, evenly spaced, centred in the lower half of the frame.

**Keyframe prompt (empty plate, shot 2)**
```
[STYLE PREFIX] Locked-off wide shot of open, smooth, swept red-brown
earth in a Ghanaian compound, the base of a mango tree trunk at the far
left edge, a low clay wall softly out of focus in the background. The
entire lower two thirds of the frame is empty, flat open ground with
nothing on it. Light from the upper left. No characters, no fruit.
```

**Ananse for shot 2:** generate him separately on a plain green background
sitting and watching ("The spider sits and turns his head slowly left to right,
following something falling, nodding gently each time, blinking"), then key him
onto the plate on the left. This keeps the mango area clean.

**Hero mango prompt (generate once)**
```
[STYLE PREFIX] A single ripe mango, plump and kidney shaped, orange-red
blush fading to yellow-green near the short stem, one small glossy leaf
on the stem, soft satin sheen, centred on a plain pure white background,
three-quarter view, soft shadow beneath.
```
Remove the background (Canva "Remove background", or `rembg`) and keep the PNG.

**Sound:** for each landing, a soft fruity "thup" (`thud.mp3`, quiet) plus the
matching `count-01.wav` to `count-10.wav` from `public/ananse/music/`. Those ten
notes **rise** with the count, so the child hears the number getting bigger.
Music ducks to about a fifth of its level while Ananse speaks.

### Scene 6 · All together, one more time · 1:40 to 2:02

**Shots**
1. 1:40 to 1:44 · Ananse turns to camera and beckons. Mood `happy`.
2. 1:44 to 1:56 · Same ten frame. A wave runs across the mangoes: each one

> One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten!

## 5. Voice, music and sound direction

### Ghanaian voice: casting and direction

**Recommended: a real Ghanaian voice actor.** Neither Piper nor the Windows
voices have a Ghanaian accent (`docs/sound.md`, issue noted there), and an
AI voice that is "nearly Ghanaian" is the thing a Ghanaian parent will notice
first. A professional voice artist in Accra or Kumasi, or a Ghanaian artist on
Voices.com or Fiverr, will record 37 short lines in under an hour.

- **Ananse:** adult male, warm and playful, a natural Ghanaian English accent
  (Akan background suits Ananse best, since he comes from Akan folklore). Must be
  able to go from a greedy "Mmm" to a whisper to a startled "Ei!". Think a fun
  uncle telling Anansesem at night.
- **Mama Yaa:** adult female, warm Ghanaian mother, two lines only. Could be
  the same session.

**Direction notes for the artist**
- Keep the accent natural; do not "neutralise" it. That is the point.
- But articulate **every number clearly and fully**: "three" and "seven" must be
  unmistakable to a three year old.
- Slow down. About 100 to 110 words a minute, slower than normal speech.
- Smile while speaking on all Ananse lines except 23 and 25.
- Say each counted number as a **complete word with a small rise**, not a list.

**Fallback: ElevenLabs.** Use the Voice Library filtered to English with a
Ghanaian or West African accent, or Voice Design with a description such as
"warm, playful middle aged Ghanaian man, Akan accent, storyteller, clear,
friendly, for a preschool cartoon". Audition three voices with lines 01, 21
and 25 (the same three lines tested in `public/ananse/audition/`), and ask a
Ghanaian parent to pick. Confirm the plan's commercial licence before release.

**Recording spec:** 48 kHz, 24 bit WAV, mono, a quiet room with soft furnishing,
mouth about 15 cm from the mic, no music while recording.

(Suno or Udio on a paid plan, which grants commercial use) can also produce

instrumental, no vocals".

cockerel once) for place, kept very low. Freesound.org filtered to CC0 for anything missing.

### Mix

- Voice is king. Dialogue peaks around -6 dBFS.
- Music ducked about 14 dB under speech; up to full between lines.
- Count notes sit just under the voice, never on top of it.
- Final loudness about **-14 LUFS** integrated (YouTube), true peak below -1 dBTP.
- Check the mix on a phone speaker: that is how most children will hear it.

## 6. Production workflow

### Recommended path: AI 3D shots + composited counting (about 7 working days, one person)

This fits the hardware: the laptop has Intel UHD 620 and no CUDA, so all heavy
generation happens in cloud tools, and the final assembly uses Remotion, which
already renders on this CPU (`docs/video.md`).

**Step 1 · Lock the script and timing (half a day)**
Approve this pack. Read the narration aloud with a stopwatch.

**Step 2 · Character and world (1 day)**
1. Generate the character sheet and expression sheet (section 2) in ChatGPT
   (gpt-image), Midjourney or Gemini. Generate 10 to 20, pick one. Check: eight
   legs, diagonal sash, red cap, not scary.
2. Show it to two or three young children and a parent. If they smile, it is approved.
3. Generate the backgrounds: the establishing shot, the empty ground plate,
   the tree low angle, the end card background.
4. Generate the hero mango and remove its background.

**Step 3 · Record the voice (1 day, mostly waiting)**
Book the voice artist with the section 4 table. Clean each take (Audacity or
Adobe Podcast Enhance), trim, name them `01.wav` to `37.wav`, measure each
duration. The durations set the final timing.

**Step 4 · Generate the shots (2 days)**
For each shot in section 3:
1. Make the keyframe still with the character sheet as the reference image.
2. Animate it with image to video (Kling, Google Veo, Runway or Hailuo), 5 to 10
   seconds, using the motion prompt plus the negative prompt.
3. Generate 3 or 4 versions of each and keep the best. Expect about 25 shots in total.

Rules that save time:
- **Always start from a still** (image to video), never text to video, so Ananse
  stays the same character.
- Generate Ananse **on green** whenever he shares the frame with counted mangoes.
- Check the legs on every frame. If he grows a ninth leg, regenerate.

**Step 5 · Lip sync (half a day)**
For the talking shots (scenes 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 close ups), run the clip and its
voice file through a lip sync tool (Kling Lip Sync, Runway, Hedra or Sync Labs).
A cartoon spider's mouth only needs to open and close in time; do not chase realism.

**Step 6 · Assemble and composite (1.5 days)**
Two choices:
- **Remotion (recommended for this repo):** make a new composition next to
  `video/ananse/lessons/count-1-to-10/`. Put each AI clip in as an
  `<OffthreadVideo>`, and draw the mangoes, numerals, hands and title with the
  same code the 2D lesson already uses, timed from the measured voice durations.
  The counting is then exact by construction and re-times itself if a line is re-recorded.
- **DaVinci Resolve (free):** if editing by hand is preferred. Put the plates on
  V1, keyed Ananse on V2, mango PNGs on V3 with keyframed drop and bounce, numerals
  as Text+ on V4. Slower, and every re-record means re-timing by hand.

**Step 7 · Music, sound and mix (half a day)**
Lay in the cue sheet (section 5), duck under voice, export at -14 LUFS.

**Step 8 · Review and export (half a day)**
Run the QA checklist below, then export:
- Master: MP4, H.264, 1920×1080, 25 fps, about 10 Mbps, AAC 192 kbps.
- Small: 1280×720 at about 2.5 Mbps for low data phones and WhatsApp.
- Vertical: 1080×1920 reframe for Shorts and Status.
- Burned in subtitles version and a separate `.srt`.
- Thumbnail: Ananse `happy` holding a mango, the numeral 10, cream background.

### Alternative path: full 3D in Blender (3 to 5 weeks)

For complete control and a reusable 3D Ananse for future films and the app:
model Ananse in Blender (a sphere body, sphere head, eight bendy legs with a
simple bone chain each, a cloth sash, cap), rig with Rigify or Auto-Rig Pro,
add shape keys for the seven moods and five mouth shapes, animate, and render in
**Eevee**. The laptop can render Eevee at 720p slowly; for 1080p use the free
SheepIt render farm or a rented GPU for a day. Worth it **after** the AI version
has proven the film works, because then every future lesson reuses the same rig.

### QA checklist (run on the final export)

- [ ] Every spoken number matches the number of mangoes on screen at that moment.
- [ ] Every numeral appears **as** its number is spoken, not before.
- [ ] Ananse has exactly eight legs in every shot.
- [ ] Light comes from the upper left in every shot.
- [ ] No AI generated text, letters or glyphs anywhere in the background.
- [ ] No people visible.
- [ ] The hand graphics have exactly five fingers each.
- [ ] Each counted number has a pause long enough for a child to repeat it.
- [ ] The voice is clear on a phone speaker; music never covers a word.
- [ ] Nothing flashes faster than three times a second (photosensitivity).
- [ ] Watched once by a Ghanaian parent and once by a child aged 3 to 6.
- [ ] All music and sound licences recorded in a `LICENCE.txt` next to the project.
- [ ] On YouTube: set as **Made for Kids**.

## 7. Risks and how they are handled

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| AI miscounts or writes fake numerals | Never asked to. All counting is composited from one mango asset. |
| Ananse looks different shot to shot | Every shot starts from a still made with the approved character sheet as reference. |
| Ananse frightens a small child | Plush materials, huge eyes, no fangs or hair, warm light; tested on children at step 2. |
| Accent sounds fake | Real Ghanaian voice artist first; AI voice only as fallback, chosen by a Ghanaian parent. |
| Counting too fast for a three year old | Scene timing follows the recorded voice; 2.2 s per number in the first count. |
| Film teaches differently from the game | Teaching lines are copied verbatim from `lesson.ts`. |
