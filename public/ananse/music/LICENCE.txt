Converted from the pack's .ogg originals to mono 64 kbit MP3, for the same
reason the sound effects were: Safari only gained Ogg support recently and this
has to play on the oldest phone in the classroom.

The steel drum family was chosen over the saxophone, chiptune and pizzicato
ones. It is warm rather than jokey, and it is the closest thing in the pack to
an instrument a Ghanaian child hears outside of school.

── The counting notes ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  count-01.mp3 .. count-10.mp3

Ours, synthesised by `tools/lesson-music.mjs`. Kenney has no scale in it and a
scale is the point: the pitch climbs with the number, so "how many" and "how
high" become the same sensation. That is how every counting song a child has
heard works, and it is a teaching device rather than decoration.

C major pentatonic, two octaves, one note per number. Pentatonic because it has
no semitones: any two of its notes sound right together in any order at any
speed, which means the counting cannot sound wrong even when a child taps ahead
of the beat.
